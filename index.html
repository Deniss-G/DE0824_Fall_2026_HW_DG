What are audio effects?

Audio effects are tools used to modify the characteristics of an audio signal. They can be implemented as hardware devices, software plug-ins, or built-in functions in digital audio workstations and mixing consoles. Audio effects may be used for technical purposes, such as controlling excessive volume or removing unwanted frequencies, as well as for creative purposes, such as adding space, movement, distortion, or unusual textures to a sound.

Audio effects can be divided into several major categories according to the aspect of the signal they modify. Dynamics processors, such as compressors, limiters, expanders, and noise gates, control the level and dynamic range of a signal. Frequency-based effects, including equalizers and filters, alter its frequency content. Time-based effects, such as delay, echo, and reverb, create repetitions or simulated acoustic spaces, while modulation effects, including chorus, flanger, phaser, and tremolo, introduce periodic changes to parameters such as pitch, amplitude, or phase.

Other important categories include distortion and saturation, which alter the waveform and add harmonic content, and pitch-processing effects, such as pitch shifters, harmonizers, and automatic pitch correction. Audio processing may also include stereo imaging, spatial effects, transient processing, and specialized restoration tools for reducing noise, clicks, hum, or other unwanted sounds. In practice, several types of effects are often combined into an effects chain, allowing engineers and musicians to shape a sound progressively from basic corrective processing to complex creative transformations.

  3. Clipping & Distortion
  4. Delay & time effects
